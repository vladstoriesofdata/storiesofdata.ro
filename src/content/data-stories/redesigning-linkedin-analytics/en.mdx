Nothing fancy and pretty easy to implement.</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>Now let&#x27;s redesign the &quot;top performing posts&quot; section.<br/></p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>We don’t want to lose any information that was available before with the redesign.<br/>For visualizing rankings, bar charts are usually a good choice - certainly better than showing individual cards ... and only 3 of them.</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p><br/>A bar chart where posts are sorted descending by impressions and where we show at least the top 20 posts would already be much more useful.<br/>Let’s add bars for engagements too so we won’t have to make any clicks if we want to see engagements.</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍<br/>For clarity, let’s add some detail. We can show the first few words of each post, since we don’t have a better way of identifying each post. <strong>We can then add data labels next to each bar. Finally, I would add the post date to help place the posts,</strong> at least mentally on a calendar.<br/>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍<br/><strong>Of course, to really help vizualise these top performing posts in context, clicking and hovering on each of them should highlight the marker on the line chart and filter the section</strong> on the right.</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p><br/></p><p>Let’s come back to impact.</p><p>When someone looks at the LinkedIn analytics page, they might be wondering<br/><br/>“How are my posts doing?”<br/><br/>With the redesign, I think we can help them answer that question in an elegant way.<br/>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍<br/>But we can do more for our users. And if we can do more, why not do more for them? Eh? ... LinkedIn?</p><p><br/></p><p>So how can we help more?</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><h2>Impact</h2><p>‍</p><p>How do you show the impact of the posts of the users?</p><p><br/></p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>This is a more loaded question and needs some definitions. How do you define impact?<br/>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍<br/>In an ideal world, we would sit together with our users and learn from them what post impact is for them.<br/><br/>Let’s assume our users either don’t know or that we know better than them.</p><p><br/></p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>No, really, for the sake of the exercise let’s assume post impact is measured by a combination of:</p><p>1. Quantitative measures</p><ul role=\"list\"><li>impressions</li><li>engagements</li><li>engagements by type</li><li>longevity<em> - </em>the number of days a post got any impressions</li><li>relevance<em> - </em>the number<em></em>of days a post got any engagements</li><li>deceleration<em> - </em>the speed with which the impressions are diminishing between the post date and current date</li><li>reach<em> - </em>how many 1st, 2nd and 3rd level relationships have engaged with the post</li></ul><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>2. Qualitative measures</p><ul role=\"list\"><li>The positions and credentials of the people who have engaged with the post</li><li>The popularity of the people who have engaged with the post</li><li>The credibility of the people who have engaged with the post</li><li>The authority of the people who have engaged with the post based on the post’s topic</li></ul><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍<br/></p><p>Get the idea? Defining impact is an exercise and a lot of ingredients can go into it. Let’s go with the above definition and mix and let’s also assume we’re not smart enough to weigh all these ingredients to calculate a singular score, similar to the <a href=\"https://www.morningstar.com/company/morningstar-ratings-faq\">Morningstar rating score</a>. <br/>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍<br/>So how do we inform our users of this impact and help them visualize it?<br/>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍<br/>Let’s take our redesigned analytics page and let’s build everything in the “advanced” analytics mode.</p><p>I will propose upgrading the same 3 sections to stay in the same design patterns.</p><h3>‍</h3><h3>‍</h3><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><h3>‍</h3><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><h3>‍</h3><p><strong>Let&#x27;s start with the bar chart.</strong></p><p>This is a good place to include all our quantitative measures. A simple table is a good way to visualize them. By adding a sorting option for each column, we will be able to see the posts by the metrics we’re most interested in.</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p><strong>Let&#x27;s continue with the right side.</strong><br/></p><p>We’ll keep the few post details, but expand the metrics to include the quantitative ones.</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>Let’s also expand the engagement section to include the number of engagements for each type.</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>Let’s also include the qualitative measures. This would be a perfect use case for an AI engine that selects the most relevant people that have engaged with the post. Let’s assume that the engine is built and we just show these people in the available space.</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p><strong>Lastly, let&#x27;s upgrade the line chart.<br/></strong></p><p>By clinking on a marker you would see the each post on the right side but, this time, we’ll also show a different marker for each post and their separate area charts.</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><p>‍</p><h3>Let’s visualize.</h3><p>The markers and areas now represent the individual post impressions.
