At Stories of Data, we believe intuition plays an important role in decision-making. Gut feeling is still data, just compressed and without any evidence attached. It is our &quot;second brain&quot; alerting us that something is off and we encourage vocalizing it. Otherwise, we might not be looking at the complete picture.</p><p class=\"paragraph-14\">Bezos, Branson and Jobs have all advocated for intuition in business. Bezos noted how delivery data can say everything&#x27;s on time, and customers can still be unhappy. What data does, is it evokes better, more nuanced questions.</p><div class=\"w-richtext\"><figure style=\"max-width:1536px\" class=\"w-richtext-align-fullwidth w-richtext-figure-type-image\"><div><img src=\"./images/c6cac3b0.png\" alt=\"\"/></div></figure><p>‍</p></div><div class=\"w-layout-grid grid-3\"><p class=\"paragraph-21\">Data-driven says</p><p class=\"paragraph-23\">Data-informed asks</p><p class=\"paragraph-22\">“Sales are down 12%. It’s better.”<br/><br/>“Revenue is growing, so the strategy is working.”</p><p class=\"paragraph-22\">“Sales are down 12%. What’s driving the decline?”<br/><br/>“Why is the CPA lower, and are we attracting the right customers?”<br/><br/>“Why do customers use Feature A, and what job are they trying to accomplish?”<br/><br/>“It gets more clicks, but does it produce better leads or revenue?”<br/><br/>“Revenue is growing, but what’s causing the growth and is it sustainable?”</p></div><p class=\"paragraph-43\">Data analysis is nothing more than an exercise on learning how to ask better questions.
