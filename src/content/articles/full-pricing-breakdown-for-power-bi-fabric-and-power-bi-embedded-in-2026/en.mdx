Ask three different consultants which product you should buy and you&#x27;ll probably get three different answers. Each one technically correct for a slightly different set of assumptions.</p><p class=\"paragraph-16\">The reason for the confusion: <strong>there isn&#x27;t one singular product</strong>.</p><p class=\"paragraph-15\">There&#x27;s <strong>Power BI</strong>. That’s Microsoft’s BI tool and almost everybody has heard about it. Then there’s <strong>Fabric</strong>, the broader data platform that Power BI lives inside, which also brings data engineering, warehousing, real-time analytics and AI into one environment. And there&#x27;s <strong>Power BI Embedded</strong>. That’s the licensing model built for organizations that want to deliver Power BI reports inside their own applications, without their audience ever touching a Microsoft product.</p><p class=\"paragraph-17\">Each has its own pricing logic, its own ceiling and its own use case. Pick the wrong one and you&#x27;re either overpaying for capacity you don&#x27;t use or hitting limits you didn&#x27;t see coming. </p><h2 class=\"heading-15\">How does Power BI per user pricing work?</h2><p class=\"paragraph-18\"><a href=\"https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/power-platform/products/power-bi/pricing\"><strong>Power BI Pro</strong></a> costs <strong>$14/user/month</strong>. It&#x27;s the baseline for anyone who wants to build a .pbix file in Power BI Desktop, publish a report to the Power BI service and share it with colleagues. Every single person who wants to view the report also needs a Pro license and because of that the price grows linearly with the number of users, even for users who only glance at a figure once a month.</p><p class=\"paragraph-20\"><em class=\"italic-text\">That&#x27;s a bit like booking a meeting room and having to purchase a ticket for every person who walks through the door, including the ones who only stopped by to check if the coffee was ready.</em></p><p class=\"paragraph-19\"><strong>Premium Per User (PPU)</strong> steps it up at <strong>$24/user/month</strong>. For that extra cost, you get datasets up to 100 GB (versus Pro&#x27;s 1 GB cap), up to 48 refreshes per day instead of 8, deployment pipelines, XMLA endpoints, AI features and paginated reports. </p><p class=\"paragraph-17\">It&#x27;s still on a per-user billing model, which means the cost still scales with every new viewer you add.</p><p class=\"paragraph-17\">Both Pro and PPU are the natural fit for small to mid-sized teams where everyone is an active participant. They start showing their limits when you want to distribute reports to a larger audience, to external users or when you need more compute power.</p><div class=\"w-richtext\"><blockquote><strong>Pro vs PPU</strong></blockquote><blockquote>PPU ($24/user/mo): All Pro features + 100 GB datasets, 48 refreshes/day</blockquote><blockquote>Pro ($14/user/mo): Build + share within org, 1 GB datasets, 8 refreshes/day</blockquote></div><h2 class=\"heading-15\">Understanding the capacity model in Fabric</h2><p class=\"paragraph-17\">Microsoft Fabric calls their computer units F SKUs. Instead of a license per user per month, you buy a dedicated number of SKUs across your entire organization, similar to how cloud computing pricing works. </p><p class=\"paragraph-17\">The pricing is hourly and billed by the second (with a one-minute minimum) through your Azure subscription. At F64 and above, users who only need to view reports no longer require individual paid licenses.</p><p class=\"paragraph-17\">Below F64, every report consumer still needs a Power BI Pro license, which means that for small capacities used purely for report distribution to a large audience, you end up paying for both the capacity and the per-user licenses.</p><p class=\"paragraph-17\">A cool thing about F SKUs is that they can be paused. You can pause capacity at night, on weekends or whenever your workloads aren&#x27;t running (and you stop paying immediately). Moreover, a 1-year reservation brings costs down by roughly 40% versus pay-as-you-go.</p><p class=\"paragraph-17\">And critically: F SKUs aren&#x27;t just Power BI hosting. They cover the entire Fabric stack: Data Factory pipelines, Spark notebooks, Data Warehouse, Lakehouse, Real-Time Intelligence, and many other features.</p><div class=\"w-richtext\"><p><strong>Key takeaway:</strong></p><blockquote>Below F64: capacity cost + Pro license for every viewer. As data complexity increases, PBI PPU feels like the normal upgrade: it’s Pro, but enhanced.</p><p class=\"paragraph-17\">Things get more complex for larger organizations. At some point the per-user billing model stops making sense and that&#x27;s when capacity starts looking attractive. P SKUs are being phased out in favor of F SKUs, which offer equivalent or greater compute at lower cost in most tiers. If you&#x27;re currently on a P SKU, it&#x27;s worth modeling an F SKU equivalent before your next renewal.</p><h3>What happens to my Power BI reports if I move to Fabric?</h3><p class=\"paragraph-17\">Nothing breaks. Fabric is backward compatible with existing Power BI content. Your .pbix files, semantic models, and workspaces migrate without rebuilding from scratch. The transition is additive, not disruptive.</p><h3>Is Power BI Embedded the same as embedding a report in an iframe?</h3><p class=\"paragraph-17\">Not exactly. You can embed a Power BI report in an iframe without A SKUs using the free embed option, but that requires the viewer to have a Microsoft account and appropriate license. A SKUs are what remove that requirement entirely, letting you serve reports to external users with no Microsoft accounts through your own application.</p><div class=\"rich-text-block-6 w-richtext\"><blockquote>Prices are approximately in the central US region.
